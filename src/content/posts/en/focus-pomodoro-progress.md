A timer that counts down from 25 minutes is easy to build. A timer that helps you understand where your attention went is more valuable.

The usual pomodoro workflow has a missing link. You start a session, finish it, and maybe feel productive. But unless the session is connected to a task, it becomes a blank block of time. After a week, the record says you focused. It does not say what moved forward.

The better workflow is simple:

1. Choose the todo before the session starts.
2. Run the focus block.
3. Save the session with the task, timestamp, and outcome.
4. Review time by project, goal, or category.

This turns focus into evidence. It also keeps todo planning honest. A task that absorbs six focus sessions is different from a task that takes one. The system should make that visible.

## Focus without context is hard to improve

Many people do not need another timer. They need a feedback loop. Which kind of work actually got attention? Which todos repeatedly stalled? Which goals received time this week?

When focus sessions are tied to todos, the review becomes practical. You can see effort, not just intention.

## The timer becomes part of the operating system

In integ.life, the pomodoro flow is not isolated from the rest of the product. It connects with todos, calendars, and goal review. That makes a session more than a countdown. It becomes a small piece of execution history.
